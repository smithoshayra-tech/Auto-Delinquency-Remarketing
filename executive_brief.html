The point of view

Fewer new loans are going seriously late than in 2009 (3.00% vs 3.48% annualized), credit mix is safer than 2006 (29% vs 44% subprime) and unemployment is 4.3%. Yet a record share of balances that go 30 days late now reaches 90+, and since mid-2024 the climb in the delinquent stock is 81% loans staying delinquent longer. That is a collections-effectiveness and remarketing-timing issue, and the 2021-2023 vintages sit on used prices that have fallen below origination levels.

What the data says

  • Inflow is 28% above 2019 (3.00% vs 2.35%) but flat since mid-2024 (up 4%).
  • Persistence rebuilt from 1.54 years (2024Q2) to 1.83; still below 2019 (2.02).
  • Broad: every age band is 21% to 33% above 2019. Not unemployment: 2009 inflow 3.24% came with 9.9% unemployment.

Where I could be wrong

  • These are industry aggregates for all lenders, not any one company's book; treat them as hypotheses for loan-level data.
  • Roll rate and persistence are ratios of aggregate flows. Used-vehicle CPI is a consumer index, not auction recovery value. The repossession share in the grid is an assumption.
  • The Philadelphia Fed argues headline delinquency overstates distress; this brief agrees on mechanics and differs on what it means for operations.
